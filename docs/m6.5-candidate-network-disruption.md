# M6.5 — Road-network disruption and settlement connectivity

**Status:** implemented and tested. **Blocked on real data:** the M6.4 road and
settlement artifacts do not exist in this checkout, because no permitted
pre-event OpenStreetMap layer does and `provenance_status` is deliberately still
`UNVERIFIED` (adopting the study extent does not authorise retrieval —
`docs/m6.15-aoi-adoption-implementation.md`). The stage runs end to end on
synthetic fixtures, reports an explicit blocker on the real inputs, and produces
no real connectivity number.

## Scope

This stage consumes the M6.4 candidate-exposure artifacts and answers the
Track B question *who may be cut off* — as a **scenario**, under a closure rule
it states explicitly.

It does:

1. build the pre-event road graph from `candidate_exposure_roads.geojson`;
2. apply a hypothetical closure rule to the roads M6.4 measured as
   candidate-intersecting;
3. compare connected components and settlement-to-hub routes against the
   untouched baseline graph;
4. report threshold sensitivity, data-coverage uncertainty and provenance.

It does **not** retrieve anything, does not run the M5 segmentation model, does
not read a DEM, and draws no conclusion about physical road damage.

## Why this is a scenario and not an observation

M6.4 measured `candidate_m`: the length of each road lying on M6.3 Sentinel-1
RTC dual-polarisation γ⁰ darkening-candidate pixels. Nothing in that
measurement establishes that such a road is impassable. `AGENTS.md` §8 is
explicit: *a flood mask intersecting a road is evidence of spatial exposure, not
automatic evidence of physical road destruction.* The M6.4 contract carries no
passability field.

So this stage does not "apply observed damage to the graph". It states a rule,
applies it, and reports the comparison:

| Scenario | Pieces removed from the graph |
|---|---|
| `baseline` | none — the graph as mapped in pre-event OSM |
| `candidate_closure` | closure units whose candidate-intersecting length reaches the threshold |
| `candidate_and_unobserved_closure` | the above, plus units whose **not-observed** length reaches it |

Every reachability number is conditional on that rule. A settlement reported
`SCENARIO_UNREACHABLE` *would* lose its modelled road route to that hub group
*if* candidate-intersecting roads were impassable. It is not a finding that a
road failed or that anybody is cut off.

## Closure unit

The threshold acts on one of two units, declared in
`configs/candidate_disruption.yaml`:

| `closure_unit` | Rule | Needs | Character |
|---|---|---|---|
| `road` (default) | a whole OSM way closes when its own `candidate_m` reaches the threshold | the M6.4 artifact only | coarse and pessimistic: 10 m of intersection closes the entire way |
| `piece` | each graph piece is re-measured against the M6.3 candidate raster with the M6.4 kernel and closes on its own `candidate_m` | `--candidate-mask` | exact at piece resolution |

`road` is the default because it is the only unit that works from the M6.4
artifact alone, and because it is the unit M6.4 actually measured. It is an
**upper bound** on disruption, not a best estimate, and a test asserts the two
units disagree in that direction.

Per-piece quantities are **never** obtained by dividing a road's `candidate_m`
between its pieces. Where along a way the intersection falls is not recorded in
the M6.4 contract, and inventing a distribution would manufacture evidence that
a specific piece was affected. If piece resolution is wanted, the raster is
re-read and the measurement is repeated.

## Graph semantics

Reused unchanged from `src/floodmap/network/graph.py`, so this stage cannot
drift from the flood-class connectivity stage:

- edges are road **pieces** between junctions; a junction is a way end, an OSM
  node shared by two ways, or the road vertex a settlement or hub attaches to;
- two ways that cross without sharing a node stay unconnected, as in OSM, so a
  bridge over a road is not a junction;
- the graph is an undirected multigraph weighted by length; `oneway` is ignored,
  because in an emergency the question is whether a road exists;
- settlements and hubs are single representative points, each attached to the
  nearest road vertex within `snap_max_m`; farther than that, no verdict.

The threshold rule itself is `floodmap.network.connectivity.removed_pieces`, and
routing is its `reach_from_hubs` / `classify` / `route_geometry` — a single
multi-source Dijkstra per scenario from a virtual source joined to every hub of
a group at zero cost.

Only the **assembly** is new. `assess_connectivity` names its outputs for a
predicted flood (`*_km_after`, "after the flood"), and an M6.5 artifact must not
describe a radar-change scenario in flood vocabulary. Statuses and reasons are
translated through a table, the same way M6.4 translates the exposure kernel's.

## Status vocabulary

| Status | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `SCENARIO_REACHABLE` | reaches a hub in both closure scenarios, by a route no longer than `detour_ratio` × baseline |
| `SCENARIO_DETOURED` | still reaches a hub under candidate closure, but the shortest route grows by at least `detour_ratio` |
| `SCENARIO_UNREACHABLE` | had a baseline route and reaches no hub of the group under candidate closure. **Modelled** road inaccessibility under the stated rule, not confirmed isolation |
| `UNREACHABLE_ONLY_IF_UNOBSERVED_CLOSED` | reachable under candidate closure, unreachable when not-observed units close too. Undetermined because of observation coverage, not modelled disruption |
| `INSUFFICIENT_DATA` | no mapped road within `snap_max_m`, no baseline route to any hub of the group, or no hub of the group near a mapped road |

A settlement with no baseline route is `INSUFFICIENT_DATA`, never unreachable:
the scenario did not cause that, and many Himalayan hamlets have no mapped road
at all.

Per-piece labels are `CLOSED_IN_CANDIDATE_SCENARIO`,
`CLOSED_ONLY_IF_UNOBSERVED_CLOSED` and `OPEN_IN_BOTH_SCENARIOS`. The last is not
a statement that the road is intact.

## Missing data is not good news

`not_observed_m` is **never** treated as unaffected road length:

- it drives the second, pessimistic scenario;
- it is reported under `coverage_uncertainty`, separately from `disruption`,
  with a note that the two must not be added or subtracted;
- a settlement whose outcome differs between the two scenarios is reported as
  `UNREACHABLE_ONLY_IF_UNOBSERVED_CLOSED`, not resolved either way.

`NO_CANDIDATE_INTERSECTION` length means the M6.3 rule did not fire there. It is
reported as such, not as intact road.

Roads entirely outside the candidate raster extent are left open in both
scenarios (`close_outside_grid_roads: false`): they were never observed by the
change detector, and closing the 25 km buffer ring that carries the routes to
out-of-AOI hubs would make the scenario meaningless. Their length is reported
under `coverage_uncertainty`. That is a stated assumption, not an observation.

## Configuration

`configs/candidate_disruption.yaml`:

```text
network.snap_max_m                 500     matches impact.yaml network.snap_max_m
network.closure_unit               road    road | piece
network.closure_min_candidate_m   10.0     matches candidate_exposure.yaml
                                           intersection.line_min_candidate_m, so the
                                           default scenario reproduces M6.4's own
                                           CANDIDATE_INTERSECTING verdict
network.sensitivity_closure_m  [10,30,100] AGENTS.md §9 threshold sensitivity
network.detour_ratio               1.5
network.close_outside_grid_roads   false
inputs.osm.allow_download          false
```

Tests assert the threshold mirrors `candidate_exposure.yaml` and the snap
distance mirrors `impact.yaml`, so the three stages cannot silently disagree
about what "intersecting" and "near a road" mean. A configuration with a single
sensitivity threshold is refused: a conclusion that cannot be shown to be
threshold-independent should not be reportable.

## Running the stage

```bash
.venv/bin/python scripts/candidate_disruption.py \
  --exposure-dir artifacts/m6_4/candidate_infrastructure_exposure \
  --osm-dir <directory holding pre-event osm_hubs.geojson> \
  --out-dir artifacts/m6_5/candidate_network_disruption
```

Piece-resolution closure, which re-measures against the raster:

```bash
.venv/bin/python scripts/candidate_disruption.py \
  --exposure-dir artifacts/m6_4/candidate_infrastructure_exposure \
  --osm-dir <osm-dir> \
  --candidate-mask artifacts/m6_3/rtc_change_candidates/rtc_change_candidates.tif \
  --valid-mask artifacts/m6_3/rtc_change_candidates/rtc_valid_mask.tif \
  --closure-unit piece \
  --out-dir artifacts/m6_5/candidate_network_disruption
```

`--exposure-dir` must contain the layout M6.4 writes:

```text
candidate_exposure_roads.geojson        required
candidate_exposure_settlements.geojson  required
candidate_exposure_summary.json         required (stage tag, analysis CRS,
                                        Sentinel-1 pair, OSM snapshot, AOI)
```

`--osm-dir` supplies `osm_hubs.geojson` plus `osm_provenance.json`. Hubs are
reference destinations rather than exposure subjects, so M6.4 produces no hub
artifact; they come from the same pre-event OSM directory, under the same
pre-event snapshot rule. Without `--osm-dir`, road disruption and
connected-component change are still reported and hub reachability is recorded
as **not assessed** — never silently replaced by a different question. Pass
`--require-hubs` to make its absence an error instead.

The analysis CRS defaults to the candidate mask's CRS recorded by M6.4, so piece
lengths and the `candidate_m` they are compared against were measured on the
same grid. A geographic CRS is refused.

### Exit codes

| Code | Meaning |
|---:|---|
| 0 | Scenario computed; artifacts written. |
| 1 | An input is invalid (wrong upstream stage tag, flood-mask exposure artifact supplied, missing contract column, unrecognised status, empty road layer, geographic CRS, post-event hub snapshot, `piece` unit without a raster). |
| 2 | Required M6.4 artifacts are absent. A blocker report is written and no connectivity number is produced. |

## Outputs

Git-ignored, under `artifacts/m6_5/candidate_network_disruption/`:

- `candidate_disruption_pieces.geojson` — every graph piece with `piece_id`,
  `u`, `v`, `osm_id`, `highway`, `length_m`, `closure_unit_length_m`,
  `candidate_m`, `not_observed_m`, `source_candidate_status`, `closure_status`
  and `routable`. Open pieces are retained and labelled, so the reader sees the
  whole modelled network and not only its closed part;
- `candidate_disruption_settlements.geojson` — per settlement: `osm_id`, `name`,
  `place`, the carried M6.4 `candidate_status`, `graph_node`,
  `snap_distance_m`, `component_baseline`,
  `component_candidate_closure`, `component_nodes_baseline`,
  `component_nodes_candidate_closure`, and per hub group `<group>_status`,
  `<group>_reason`, `<group>_hub_baseline`, `<group>_km_baseline`,
  `<group>_hub_candidate_closure`, `<group>_km_candidate_closure`,
  `<group>_km_candidate_and_unobserved_closure`;
- `candidate_disruption_routes.geojson` — shortest-path lines per settlement,
  hub group and scenario (`baseline`, `candidate_closure`);
- `candidate_disruption_summary.json` — closure rule, graph statistics,
  per-scenario disruption, road-length accounting, coverage uncertainty,
  settlement statuses, threshold sensitivity, assumptions and limitations;
- `candidate_disruption_provenance.yaml` — `settlement_connectivity` when hub
  reachability was assessed, `road_disruption` when it was not, linking the
  Sentinel-1 pair, OSM snapshot, closure unit and config version;
- `candidate_disruption_blocker.json` — written **instead** of the above when a
  required input is absent.

GeoJSON is written in EPSG:4326 so it opens directly in QGIS.

### Reading component fields

`component_baseline` and `component_candidate_closure` are labels **within one
scenario**, ordered largest first. They are not identities across scenarios: a
settlement that stays in the largest surviving component keeps label `0` even
though the network it can reach has shrunk. `component_nodes_*` is the
comparable quantity, and the summary counts
`settlements.component_shrunk_under_candidate_closure` from it. A test pins this
distinction, because the obvious misreading is the dangerous one.

## Blocker and the precise next step

M6.5 is blocked on the same missing input as M6.4, one stage further down.
Obtaining it is an OSM retrieval, and `AoiProvenanceStatus.UNVERIFIED` "permits
geometry inspection and catalogue metadata analysis only; it must not be used to
authorise downloads" (`src/floodmap/acquisition/aoi.py`).
`AreaOfInterest.authorises_download` is `false`. The gate was not bypassed and
the AOI status was not changed.

To unblock, in order:

1. **Obtain a human authorisation for pre-event OSM retrieval.** The extent
   question is closed: M6.15 adopted the human-approved corridor
   `trishuli-corridor-hydrorivers-v10-m615-2ae4183f`, whose producer, source,
   construction parameters and digests are recorded in its provenance sidecar.
   `provenance_status` stays `unverified` so that adoption does not itself
   authorise retrieval; the missing decision is a separate, explicit OSM
   authorisation for this AOI. M6.5 needs M6.4 to run first and supplies no
   substitute for it.
2. **Fetch pre-event OSM** for an explicit pre-event snapshot, using the
   existing adapter, run without `--mask`:

   ```bash
   .venv/bin/python scripts/impact.py \
     --aoi-id <aoi-id> --aoi-bbox <min_lon> <min_lat> <max_lon> <max_lat> \
     --event-date 2026-08-26 --out <osm-dir>
   ```

   That writes `osm_roads.geojson`, `osm_settlements.geojson`,
   `osm_hubs.geojson` and `osm_provenance.json`.
3. **Run M6.4** with `--osm-dir <osm-dir>` to produce
   `candidate_exposure_roads.geojson` and
   `candidate_exposure_settlements.geojson`.
4. **Run this stage** with `--exposure-dir` at the M6.4 output and `--osm-dir`
   at the pre-event OSM directory. No change to this stage is required.

A real Trishuli connectivity analysis additionally needs a judgement that is
**not** a code change: whether the `road` closure unit's whole-way closure is
acceptable for a corridor whose trunk road is tens of kilometres long, or
whether `piece` resolution against the real candidate raster is required before
any settlement verdict is reported. The code supports both; which one the report
stands behind is a scientific decision.

## Limitations

Carried in every summary and provenance record:

- Closure is a stated scenario, not an observation. M6.3 candidates are
  rule-based Sentinel-1 RTC γ⁰ darkening evidence, and nothing in the M6.4
  contract establishes that a candidate-exposed road is impassable.
- Radar darkening has causes other than inundation, and inundation under
  vegetation or in built-up areas can be missed, so the closure set carries both
  false positives and false negatives.
- A `SCENARIO_UNREACHABLE` verdict is modelled road inaccessibility under the
  stated rule. It is not confirmed isolation of a settlement or of any person.
- Not-observed road length is missing information, reported under
  `coverage_uncertainty` and never counted as unaffected road.
- Road, settlement and hub completeness is limited by OpenStreetMap coverage at
  the pre-event snapshot; unmapped roads cannot carry a route and unmapped
  settlements cannot appear.
- The graph ignores road capacity, surface, gradient, seasonal passability,
  bridge load limits and landslide risk, all of which matter in a Himalayan
  corridor.
- Routes are shortest paths by length, not travel time, and ignore direction.
- Settlement and hub positions are single representative points, so a
  settlement's built-up extent and multiple access roads are not represented.
- Footpaths and footbridges are not in the road graph, so foot access is not
  modelled.
- AOI provenance remains `UNVERIFIED`, so every count is relative to an extent
  whose origin is not established.
- No DEM-based flood-path reasoning, segmentation checkpoint, validation product
  (EMSR927, Copernicus EMS, UNOSAT) or post-event OSM is read by this stage.
