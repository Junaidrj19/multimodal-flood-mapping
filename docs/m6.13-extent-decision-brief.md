## 0. Summary of what the evidence now shows

```text
CONDITIONAL GO — human extent decision required
```

Three findings change the decision from the one M6.12 framed.

1. **Candidate H2 (index 70) has no hydrological justification.** The corridor's Strahler order
   profile is exactly three segments: order 5 at indices **1–49**, order 6 at **50–80**, order 7 at
   **81–97**. Indices 50 and 81 are the order-transition boundaries. **Index 70 is interior to the
   order-6 segment** — its +40.3 % catchment jump comes from a tributary of lower order, so the
   order does not increment. It is an arbitrary point inside a homogeneous reach whose only
   distinction is that it yields a smaller polygon than index 81. That is precisely the reasoning
   the brief forbids.

2. **The three candidates as specified are the *first* reach of the next-order river, not the last
   reach of the current one.** Index 50 already carries the merged 11,651.7 km² channel; the upper
   stem ends at index **49** (6,635.2 km²). The same applies at 70→69 and 81→80. If the intent is
   "end where the upper Trishuli ends", the terminus is index 49, and that distinction is 3.293 km
   of centreline and 6.43 km² of AOI.

3. **The 2,000 m buffer refusal recorded in M6.10 §3.2 — and repeated in my own M6.12 §4.2 — is
   wrong about the geometry.** Measured through `buffer_corridor` itself, the 2 km corridor is a
   **valid single Polygon at every candidate index**, from 249.2939 km² at index 32 to 775.2976 km²
   at index 96. The 3-part MultiPolygon with 1,315,257.6 m² detached occurs **only at prefix index
   1 (1.643 km along)** and vanishes at index 2. It aborts the whole incremental walk, which is why
   the sensitivity run recorded a refusal. The mandatory `{500, 1000, 2000}` m sensitivity set is
   therefore **computable** for every candidate except A — it was never geometrically impossible.

What has **not** changed: both AOI files are byte-intact, all acquisition gates are closed, no
external data was retrieved, and the decision itself remains the human's.

## 2. Policy comparison

### 2.1 Which criteria are which

Conflating these is how the circular terminus arrived in the first place, so they are separated
before any option is scored.

| Class | Criteria | What it can and cannot settle |
|---|---|---|
| **Scientific** | Independence from the replaced artifact; no selection on the outcome; reproducibility; disclosure of what the boundary means | Can disqualify an option (C0 is disqualified). Cannot by itself choose among several non-circular options |
| **Hydrological** | Strahler-order transition; river-order class (`ORD_CLAS`); catchment area; tributary dominance at the confluence; flood-regime homogeneity | Can identify *which* boundaries are objective features of the river network. Cannot determine how much river the study should cover |
| **Observational** | Swath coverage; same-track pairing; valid-pixel fraction; clearance from the swath edge; delivered raster coverage | Can tell you where the instrument **can** see. **Cannot tell you where the flood was, and must never be read as event extent** |
| **Operational** | Raster volume and compute; UTM distortion; single-`Polygon` AOI contract; catalogue query URL length; acquisition authorization; regeneration and test churn | Can price an option. Cannot justify one |

Two rules follow, and both are applied below:

- **Scene coverage is not event extent.** The scene footprint tells us the orbit-85 pair observed
  44,246.4 km²; it says nothing about where the 26 August flood went. Option A's terminus is a
  *larger* instrument boundary, not a scientific one.
- **A confluence is not a valid boundary merely because it yields a smaller polygon.** This
  disqualifies H2 on its own terms (§1.2), and it is the reason the order profile — not the
  catchment-jump size — is the criterion used.

## 3. The inset and the buffer

### 3.1 Why the 250 m inset fails against the RTC crop and succeeds against the scene footprint

**Purpose, from M6.9 §4.2.** The inset exists so that "the study area's downstream end is a
*scientific* end rather than an *instrument* end" — it holds the corridor clear of the swath edge,
where geometry and radiometry degrade and where a flood boundary read from the image would be an
artefact of the image.

**Against the delivered RTC crop it is unsatisfiable.** Measured prefix by prefix, the east
clearance to the window edge collapses at the second reach and never recovers:

| Prefix index | Along corridor | West | South | **East** | North | In window | In 250 m inset |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 0.103 km | 31,319.1 m | 47,322.3 m | **261.8 m** | 865.9 m | yes | **yes** |
| 1 | 1.643 km | 30,891.9 m | 45,615.5 m | **71.1 m** | 865.9 m | yes | **no** |
| 2 | 3.338 km | 29,795.4 m | 45,227.9 m | **71.1 m** | 865.9 m | yes | no |
| 7 | 11.963 km | 27,644.1 m | 37,407.0 m | **71.1 m** | 865.9 m | yes | no |

The binding edge is **east, at the upstream end**, from 1.643 km onward. Downstream truncation
cannot fix an upstream violation, so with the inset applied the walk retains one reach — 0.103 km,
0.2062 km². The rule does not shift the terminus; it destroys the corridor.

**The reason is the footprint, not the inset.** The RTC crop is the old AOI's envelope padded
17.7–468.9 m. A 250 m inset of a boundary that hugs the study area by 17.7 m on one side is
necessarily empty near that side. **The dropped inset and the substituted footprint are one defect**
— M6.10 substituted the crop for the scene footprint, which made M6.9's inset arithmetically
impossible, and the inset disappeared without the refusal being recorded (while M6.10 §3.2
continued to refer to "the inset that defined it").

**Against the scene footprint it is free.** Clearances to the common annotation-grid footprint:

| Option | Clearance | 250 m | 500 m | 1,000 m |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| C0 (index 32) | 10,860.4 m | satisfied | satisfied | satisfied |
| H1 / H2 / H3 | **6,763.1 m** | satisfied (27× margin) | satisfied | satisfied |
| A (index 96) | 703.0 m | satisfied (2.8×) | satisfied | **fails** |

For every hydrological candidate the inset is satisfied with a 27× margin and has **zero effect on
the terminus**. For A it is satisfied but is the active constraint at 1,000 m.

### 3.2 What the inset should be defined relative to

| Reference | Verdict |
|---|---|
| **The delivered raster crop** | **Reject.** It is derived from the AOI (circular), and a 250 m inset of it is empty near the study area — this is the measured failure above |
| **The study boundary itself** | **Reject.** Self-referential: insetting a boundary from itself expresses no constraint |
| **The acquisition footprint — the same-track pair's common swath, from the products' own geolocation** | **Adopt.** This is what the inset was written to protect against (M6.9 §4.2) and the only reference under which it is both meaningful and measurable |

**One structural improvement the evidence supports.** Under a coverage-bounded policy the inset is
part of the *terminus definition*, which is what coupled the AOI to one scene pair. Under a
hydrological policy the terminus is asserted from the river network, and the inset becomes an
**acceptance gate**: assert the terminus, then verify the buffered corridor clears the swath edge
by ≥ 250 m, and fail loudly if it does not. That separates "where the study area is" from "whether
the instrument can see it" — which is the separation whose absence produced the circularity. H1,
H2 and H3 all pass such a gate by 6,763.1 m; A passes by 703.0 m; C0's gate cannot be evaluated
against the crop at all.

**Correction to the 2,000 m MultiPolygon finding.** M6.10 §3.2 recorded 2,000 m as refused because
"the buffered geometry is a 3-part `MultiPolygon` (1,315,257.6 m² outside the largest part) at the
first hairpin, so it cannot satisfy the single-`Polygon` AOI contract", and my own M6.12 §4.2
repeated it. Measured:

```text
prefix index 0  (0.103 km)  -> Polygon,      1 part
prefix index 1  (1.643 km)  -> MultiPolygon, 3 parts, 1,315,257.6 m² detached   <-- the refusal
prefix index 2  (3.338 km)  -> Polygon,      1 part
...
every index 2-96            -> Polygon,      1 part
```

The detached lobe is centred at `85.362364, 28.281565`, beside the main part at
`85.382459, 28.262632`, with a third zero-area sliver — a **flat-cap × hairpin interaction at a
1.6 km prefix** where the terminal cross-section slices through the buffer of the line's earlier
run. It is not a property of any candidate corridor. Because `buffer_corridor` raises and
`select_coverage_bounded_corridor` evaluates every prefix, the exception aborts the entire
selection, which is why the sensitivity row reads as a refusal.

Two consequences:

1. **The claim that a 2 km corridor is geometrically impossible on this alignment is false.**
   Widening to 2 km is a policy and acquisition question, not a contract impossibility.
2. **M6.10's second reason was the real one, and only for the crop.** At 2 km no index — including
   32 — fits inside the delivered RTC window, confirming "a 2 km half-width would exceed the
   products' northern edge". Against the scene footprint, 2 km fits for C0, H1, H2 and H3.

**Code observation (not acted on).** The single-`Polygon` guard is correct as a check on the
*delivered* AOI, but applying it inside the walk converts a transient intermediate geometry into a
total failure. A guard on the final geometry — or a skip-rather-than-abort in the walk — would have
made the mandatory sensitivity set computable from the start. This is a one-function change in
`src/floodmap/hydrology/hydrorivers.py`; **it was not made.**

**What remains unmeasured.** M6.9 §5.1 required per-buffer **exposure** (M6.4-style) and
**settlement** (M6.5-style) counts for all three widths. None exists at any width, because M6.4 and
M6.5 produced no numbers at all. Area sensitivity is now complete; **result sensitivity is not**,
and it stays blocked on authorized pre-event OSM. The buffer must be ratified with that stated as a
limitation, not treated as satisfied by the area table.

### 4.2 Recommendation

**Recommended: H1-above — terminate at index 49, reach `40801674`, the last reach of the
Strahler-order-5 upper stem.** 105.858 km centreline, 206.1509 km² at a 1,000 m buffer.

The evidence, in the order it carries weight:

1. **It is the only boundary that is simultaneously non-circular and an objective river-network
   feature matching the documented event scope.** The challenge, as recorded in `README.md`
   §1 (lines 24-26), describes the disaster as occurring "along the **Bhote Koshi–Trishuli
   corridor**". The word Narayani appears nowhere in the repository's scope language. Index 49 is
   the downstream end of the single homogeneous order-5 reach that runs from the border — 49 of 49
   reaches at `ORD_STRA` 5 — and the first reach beyond it carries a co-equal 5,009.5 km² tributary
   (75 % of the stem). "The upper stem ends where a river of its own order joins it" is
   reproducible from a provider attribute, requires no river name, and depends on neither the old
   AOI nor the instrument.
2. **It satisfies M6.9 §4.2's actual purpose with a 27× margin.** 6,763.1 m clearance from the
   swath edge means the terminus is unambiguously not instrument-bounded — the property A cannot
   claim at 703.0 m and C0 cannot claim at all.
3. **It makes the mandatory sensitivity set computable.** `{500, 1000, 2000}` m all produce valid
   single Polygons inside the scene footprint with the 250 m inset satisfied. A fails this.
4. **It is the least costly of the non-circular options on every operational axis** — highest
   delivered coverage (63.4 %), lowest UTM distortion (0.207 m per 1 km), smallest area (1.63× the
   historical corridor, against A's 3.17×).
5. **H2 is excluded on its own terms** (no order boundary, §1.2), and **H3 and A are excluded from
   a *recommendation*** because both cross into the order-7 trunk, which is a scope expansion
   beyond the documented event description that only a human can authorize.

**Assumptions this recommendation makes explicit — any one of them, if rejected, changes it:**

- That the project's event scope is the Bhote Koshi–Trishuli corridor as README §1 states, and that
  extending the study area into a higher-order trunk requires separate authorization.
- That a Strahler-order transition is an acceptable proxy for "where the upper river ends", given
  that HydroRIVERS carries no name attribute and no local dataset establishes the river-name
  boundary.
- That a 36.6 % unobserved AOI is acceptable **only** if new raster coverage is authorized, or the
  partial observation is disclosed in the AOI provenance and on the dashboard.
- That 1,000 m remains the buffer. The recommendation is independent of this: the terminus is
  invariant at 500 m, 1,000 m and 2,000 m.

**Trade-offs accepted, stated plainly:**

- **Index 49 is a 36.6 %-unobserved AOI today.** This is the recommendation's main cost and the
  reason H3/B4 below is a genuine decision rather than a formality. An AOI polygon that asserts
  105.858 km of corridor while imagery exists for 63.4 % of it will produce exposure and
  connectivity numbers for the upper part only. Presenting that without prominent disclosure would
  imply analytical coverage the data does not have — an `AGENTS.md` §16 violation. Either acquire
  the remaining coverage or disclose the split explicitly on every surface.
- **It forgoes C0's 100 % coverage and 0.091 m distortion.** Deliberately: C0's boundary is not
  independent of the artifact it replaces.
- **The order-5 criterion is a hydrographic proxy, not a named-river boundary.** A reviewer may
  reasonably prefer the catchment-class change at index 81, which is the stronger *hydrological*
  signal (`ORD_CLAS` 4→3) but the weaker *scope* fit.
- **It is a new geometry milestone, not an adoption.** New bytes, new digest, new `aoi_id`; the
  currently pinned `941a4079…0564b586` artifact would be superseded, not configured.
- **A remains the right choice if the project prefers a mechanical rule** over a hydrological
  judgement, and accepts a 32.7 %-observed, order-7-crossing, 400.06 km² corridor whose 2 km
  sensitivity case cannot be computed.
