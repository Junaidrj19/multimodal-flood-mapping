## 2. Reach selection (Stage 2)

### 2.1 Method

The whole selection is a **downstream walk**, and that is the point. At a
junction, the merged channel is the one whose `NEXT_DOWN` the upstream reach
points to, so a downstream walk cannot enter a tributary and needs no
branch-resolution heuristic. The only branch choice in the procedure would be
*upstream* of the chosen start — which is outside the corridor. Tributary
exclusion is therefore structural, not a filtering step that could silently
misfire.

Terminal rules (both recorded in the artifact, neither inferred at run time):

```text
START   the HydroRIVERS reach nearest a documented place on the river
        (Rasuwagadhi border crossing), cut at the perpendicular through that
        place; the perpendicular offset is recorded (8 mm here)
COURSE  follow NEXT_DOWN; no branch choice exists on this walk
TERMINUS the furthest downstream reach whose ENTIRE buffered corridor lies
        inside the intersection of the delivered product footprints
TIE-BREAK (where a fork is ever encountered) larger UPLAND_SKM → higher
        ORD_STRA → lower HYRIV_ID, fixed before the run
```

The old AOI's north-east terminus was *not* used as an input, and the corridor
is not clipped to the old bounding box.

### 2.3 Downstream terminal — evidence, and a correction

**Both delivered RTC products share one identical extent:**
85.050085–85.394451 E, 27.839799–28.291785 N. They were clipped to the same
window (3,313 × 4,961 px in EPSG:32645), so their intersection *is* their union.
The corridor therefore has to fit inside that window — 64.2 km of main stem, not
the 100+ km that the pair's swath geometry would nominally allow.

> **Correction to earlier statements in this milestone.** M6.9 §4.2's "≈ 102 km
> at 290°" and an interim measurement made earlier in this session (**210.5 km**)
> were computed against *scene/swath* geometry and a point-in-footprint test.
> The delivered *products* are clipped far more tightly than the swath, and the
> products are what M6.2–M6.5 actually analysed. The footprint used here is the
> true raster extent of the two delivered RTC GeoTIFFs, and the rule requires the
> whole buffered corridor to be inside it. Both earlier figures are superseded.

The walk itself continues 1,380 km down the Trishuli → Narayani → Ganges system
(`NEXT_DOWN == 0` at the Bay of Bengal drainage), which is why a coverage
terminus is required at all: without one the AOI would be a continental river.

Cross-checks that the path is the backbone and not a tributary:

* `ORD_CLAS` (documented in §3.2 as *"the main stem of a river from source to
  sink"*) is 5 on the two headwater branches at the border and decreases along
  the trunk; the network merges the merged channel at every junction, so the walk

### 2.5 Middle-stem context (recorded, not adopted)

The main stem's upland area crosses 5,000 km² near the terminus, just above the
confluence with a 672.8 km² tributary. That confluence is a plausible
hydrological boundary for a larger AOI, and it is **not** used: the terminus
rule is coverage, which is measurable and reproducible, whereas "where the
Trishuli stops being the Trishuli" would be a naming claim the data cannot
settle. It is recorded here so a future decision has the measured facts.

## 3. Construction (Stage 3)

### 3.1 Steps

```text
1  load the delivered geodatabase (GDAL/pyogrio); attributes only, no repair
2  find the reach nearest the reference, in metres (EPSG:32645), not in degrees
3  walk NEXT_DOWN to the end of the network
4  merge the ordered reaches into one LineString (UnionFind by linemerge)
5  reproject that line to EPSG:32645                       ← before any metric op
6  cut it at the perpendicular through the reference        (exact projection, not
                                                             snapped to a vertex)
7  walk downstream, buffering the prefix at each reach end, and keep the last
   prefix whose polygon is fully `within` the product footprint; stop at the
   first that is not (recorded)
8  reproject the polygon to EPSG:4326 for delivery; do not round coordinates
9  validate, measure, hash, write geometry + provenance
```

**No simplification and no validity repair.** The AOI is hash-pinned, so any
vertex-count change is a geometry change, and an invalid input is a finding to
report rather than something to fix quietly. `is_valid` is **true**;
`explain_validity` → `Valid Geometry`.

**One defect found and fixed during construction (disclosed, not hidden).**
Consecutive duplicate coordinates appear at reach boundaries and at a cut placed
exactly on a vertex. A zero-length segment carries no shape, but buffering one
with a *flat* cap makes GEOS return a `MultiPolygon` containing a degenerate
extra part — which the AOI contract refuses, for a reason unrelated to the
corridor. Repeated vertices are now dropped before buffering (`drop_repeated_vertices`),
which removes no vertex that describes shape and leaves the path length unchanged.
The delivered geometry was **identical before and after** that fix (the same
SHA-256), so the published artifact never contained the defect; only the failure
mode at 2,000 m changed. A regression test pins it.

### 3.2 Buffer and CRS

| Element | Decision | Justification |
|---|---|---|
| Source CRS | EPSG:4326 (verified, §1.2) | provider delivery CRS |
| Construction CRS | **EPSG:32645** (UTM 45N) | known meridional scale factor over the corridor envelope 0.99990848–1.00005136 ⇒ a 1,000 m buffer is 999.91–1,000.05 m on the ground, worst case **0.092 m**; one zone spans the whole 85.07–85.39 E extent; already the project's 10 m analysis grid |
| Buffering in degrees | rejected | 1 km = 0.00904° latitude but 0.01017° longitude at 28° N ⇒ **1.125× anisotropic**, and the corridor's width would depend on bearing |
| Buffer distance | **1,000 m** (primary) | matches the M6.2–M6.5 corridor width, so the replacement is comparable rather than a different experiment; ≈ 2.2 × the 15 arc-second source cell (460.7 m N–S, 409.5 m E–W at this latitude) |
| Caps | **flat** | a round cap bulges one radius (1 km) beyond each terminal — ≈ 1.57 km² per end — moving the corridor past the endpoint the provenance claims and pushing the coverage-bounded terminus back outside the inset that defined it. Flat caps end the corridor exactly at the terminal cross-section |
| Joins | round | bends in the source line must not develop notches; `quad_segs=32` |
| `quad_segs` | **32** | measured area convergence on this corridor: 125.77 / 125.89 / 125.92 km² at 8 / 16 / 32 — 32 removes a ~0.1 % under-measurement that would otherwise be inherited by every exposure count |
| Delivery CRS | EPSG:4326 | the only CRS `AreaOfInterest` accepts |

**Sensitivity (measured, recorded in the artifact):**

| Buffer | Terminus | Centreline | Area | Coverage |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 500 m | `40799268` | 64.11 km | 63.6163 km² | 1.000 |
| **1,000 m** | **`40799268`** | **64.11 km** | **126.2106 km²** | **1.000** |
| 2,000 m | — | — | — | **refused**: the buffered geometry is a 3-part `MultiPolygon` (1,315,257.6 m² outside the largest part) at the first hairpin, so it cannot satisfy the single-`Polygon` AOI contract; independently, a 2 km half-width would also exceed the products' northern edge, which lies only ~1.4 km above the reference |

That last row is a recorded refusal, not a silently narrowed corridor. Two
distinct things stand behind it: the products do not observe a 4 km-wide corridor
here, and GEOS cannot express this alignment's 2 km buffer as one polygon at all.
Widening the corridor is therefore an **acquisition** decision (a larger product
window), not a buffering parameter that can be turned up.

### 5.1 Why "traceable geometry" does not mean "verified AOI"

`AoiProvenanceStatus.VERIFIED` is the single switch that makes
`AreaOfInterest.authorises_download` true. The new corridor's *origin* is now
established, but two things are still operator decisions:

1. **the extent itself** — a main-stem-only, coverage-bounded corridor is a
   defensible analytical choice, not a ratified study area;
2. **the buffer** — 1,000 m is carried over for comparability and is unsupported
   by any operator decision (M6.9 §8.4).

Setting `verified` would open the acquisition gate as a side effect of a
geometry task. The artifact therefore records `provenance_status: unverified`
and `adoption: not_ratified`, and the tests pin both.

### 5.2 What adoption would take

A deliberate, reviewable change — no geometry work, no new hashes:

```diff
 configs/data.yaml
-  geojson_path: "configs/aoi/trishuli-corridor-UNVERIFIED.geojson"
-  sha256: "9035eeee025f311be7f16af1d23737a46caee20eb0b2a8dfa144d111b9362610"
-  aoi_id: "trishuli-corridor-UNVERIFIED-9035eeee"
+  geojson_path: "configs/aoi/trishuli-corridor-hydrorivers-v10.geojson"
+  sha256: "941a4079d3f317e260a91560eaebdd019ab5cf59c87f82e8e6165c7b0564b586"
+  aoi_id: "trishuli-corridor-hydrorivers-v10-941a4079"
   (provenance_status stays "unverified" until the extent and buffer are ratified)
```

`tests/test_aoi_hydrorivers_corridor.py::…the_acquisition_configuration_still_points_at_the_old_aoi`
is the assertion that must change with it, so the switch cannot happen as a side
effect.

### 5.3 Staleness of earlier artifacts

M6.2–M6.6 were computed inside the old corridor's *bounding box*, which contains
the whole derived AOI (the new corridor is 96 % covered by the old one, and both
sit inside the same 3,313 × 4,961 RTC window). Their rasters therefore still
cover this AOI, but their per-AOI counts describe the old polygon. They are not
edited here, and no claim in this document is derived from them.

## 7. Remaining limitations

1. **Main stem only.** Tributaries are excluded by the approved policy, so a
   flood that propagated up a tributary valley (the Tadi, the Langtang) is outside
   this AOI. This is a scope decision and is disclosed in the dashboard's AOI
   provenance, not only here.
2. **The downstream end is an instrument boundary.** The corridor stops where the
   delivered products stop observing, 64.2 km from the border. The river — and
   the flood — continues beyond it. Extending the AOI requires re-acquiring
   products with a larger window, which is an acquisition decision this milestone
   does not make.
3. **Corridor width is unratified.** 1,000 m is carried over for comparability;
   at 2,000 m no fully-covered corridor exists against these products, so the
   choice is bounded by the data as well as by judgement.
4. **Source precision.** HydroRIVERS is a 15 arc-second (~460 m at this latitude)
   delineation derived from SRTM, not a surveyed channel. A 1 km half-width is
   ~2.2 source cells; edge exposure must be read with that in mind.
5. **The reference is a place, not a station.** The Rasuwagadhi coordinate carries
   ~150 m site uncertainty even though it falls within 8 mm of the mapped stem —
   the exactness is not evidence of river position (see §2.2). The corridor starts
   at the perpendicular through that coordinate; ±150 m at the upstream end
   changes nothing material downstream.
6. **Naming is not asserted.** No reach name is claimed. The identification rests
   on topology (the walk), area, the documented border crossing, and the
   published description of the confluence — not on a name attribute the dataset
   does not have.
7. **Adoption is outstanding** (§5.1–5.2). Until an operator ratifies the extent
   and the buffer, `configs/data.yaml` still configures the unverified corridor
   and the acquisition gate stays closed.
8. **M6.2–M6.6 artifacts are stale against this AOI** (§5.3) and were not
   recomputed.
9. **Nothing here validates the flood.** Reach selection and buffering define a
   study area; they make no claim about the event, the water, or any damage.
