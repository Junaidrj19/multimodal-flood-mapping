# M6.12 — AOI Boundary Independence and Reconciliation

**Type:** read-only investigation. No geometry, configuration, artifact, gate or test was modified.
**Date:** 2026-10-17
**Scope:** resolve the M6.11 NO-GO finding that the HydroRIVERS corridor's downstream terminus
depends on the delivered RTC raster window, which traces the old unverified AOI's envelope.
Establish whether a defensible, independently justified corridor extent exists.

## 1. Executive verdict

```text
CONDITIONAL GO — specific evidence or human decisions remain
```

**The circularity is breakable, and this milestone broke it.** Independent footprint evidence
exists on disk, was not used by M6.9 or M6.10 in the form required, and yields a downstream
terminus that owes nothing to the replaced artifact:

| | Terminus | Reaches | Centreline | Area @ 1 km | Coverage |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Current (RTC crop)** | `40799268` | 33 | 64.105 km | 126.2106 km² | 1.000000 |
| **Scene footprint, SAFE annotation** | `40813406` | **97** | **205.658 km** | **400.0600 km²** | 1.000000 |
| Scene footprint, CDSE `GeoFootprint` | `40813406` | 97 | 205.658 km | 400.0600 km² | 1.000000 |
| Either footprint, 250 m inset restored | `40813406` | 97 | 205.658 km | 400.0600 km² | 1.000000 |

The scene-footprint terminus is **invariant** across two independently recorded footprints that
differ by 844.4 km² of symmetric area, and invariant under the 250 m inset M6.9 specified. It is
therefore a reproducible result, not an artefact of one footprint record. The brief's stop
condition — "if neither option can be justified from available evidence… stop rather than
inventing a terminus" — **does not trigger**.

**Why this is not a GO.** Three things remain, and none of them is a computation:

1. **Which independent boundary to adopt is a human decision with a 3.2× consequence.** The
   scene-footprint corridor is 205.658 km and 400.0600 km², against 64.105 km and 126.2106 km²
   today. Its terminus sits at an upland catchment of **31,929.0 km²** versus 5,464.2 km² — the
   corridor leaves the Trishuli and runs the Narayani trunk past three major confluences. A larger
   instrument boundary is still an instrument boundary; it is more defensible than the current one
   only because it is not circular, not because it is hydrologically meaningful.
2. **Option B's data coverage cannot be validated without new retrieval authorization.** Only
   **32.7 %** (130.6416 of 400.0600 km²) of the scene-bounded corridor lies inside the delivered
   rasters. Confirming valid RTC pixels over the rest requires opening the Planetary Computer
   COGs over the network, which this milestone is forbidden to do.
3. **The governance records M6.9 §8.6–8.7 require are still absent** — no HydroRIVERS approval
   record, no named operator. Unchanged by this milestone and unchangeable by it.

**One M6.11 finding is corrected upward, not down.** M6.11 inferred that restoring the 250 m inset
"would have materially moved the terminus". Measured: against the RTC crop it does not move the
terminus, it **destroys the corridor** — the walk collapses to one reach, 0.103 km, 0.2062 km².
The mechanism is the *east* raster edge at the upstream end, not the south edge downstream. The
dropped inset and the substituted footprint are therefore **one defect, not two** (§4.1).

## 4. Phase 3 — The 250 m inset and the buffer

### 4.1 The inset — traced, quantified, and reclassified

**Origin.** M6.9 §4.2 specifies the terminus as "the last reach whose 1 km-buffered, flat-capped
geometry lies entirely inside the common footprint of the selected same-track pair, **inset by an
additional 250 m margin**", repeated as §6.2 Step 3. The rationale is stated in the same section:

> A flood extent read from a scene is bounded by the image edge, not by the flood. Ending the
> corridor before the image edge is the only way the study area's downstream end is a *scientific*
> end rather than an *instrument* end. — M6.9 §4.2

So the inset exists to hold the corridor clear of the **swath edge**, where geometry and radiometry
degrade and where a flood boundary would be an artefact of the image. M6.9 §5.1 adds the
consistency requirement that flat caps are needed precisely so a round cap does not "defeat the
inset".

**Does it apply to the chosen extent policy?** It depends on the footprint, and the two answers are
opposite.

"the specified rule cannot be evaluated against this footprint" — in the same style as the 2,000 m

refer to "the inset that defined it".
