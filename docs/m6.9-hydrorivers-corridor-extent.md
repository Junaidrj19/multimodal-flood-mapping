### 1.2 What this investigation establishes

Five new, measured facts decide the shape of the problem. All are reproducible
from artifacts already in this checkout; none required a download.

1. **The existing Sentinel-1 raster footprint is not a sensor limit.** The two
   RTC rasters are a 3,313 × 4,961 @ 10 m crop in `EPSG:32645` whose bounds
   match the **reprojected bounding box of the unverified AOI** to within
   8.1 m (§3.1). The real Sentinel-1 IW product is 25,769 × 16,732 samples
   (§3.1). The footprint is therefore derived from the same unverified polygon
   the replacement is meant to replace: **it carries no independent information
   about the intended reach and cannot define the corridor.** It is a
   *coverage artefact*, not a coverage constraint.
2. **The actual sensor constraint is enormous.** The common footprint of the
   event-window same-track pair covers **43,704.7 km²**; the corridor is
   **0.30 %** of it (§3.2). Along the corridor's terminal axis the common
   footprint extends **155.7 km upstream** and **27.4 km downstream** (§3.3).
3. **Downstream room is direction-dependent, not a single number.** Measured
   from the downstream terminus: **11.85 km** due south, 13.4 km at 200°,
   17.95 km at 220°, **27.1 km at 235°**, 63.3 km at 250°, **91.6 km at 270°**,
   ≈ **102 km at 290°** (§3.3). Where the river actually goes decides the limit,
   which is why the downstream terminus must be evaluated against the real reach
   geometry at construction time rather than fixed now.
4. **The existing downstream termination is an unexplained oblique cut, and the
   upstream termination is not cut at all.** One 2,803.48 m straight chord at
   bearing 341.71° closes the south-western end; the north-eastern end has no
   straight segment longer than 200 m (§3.4). Buffer-then-truncate, with the
   truncation undocumented.
5. **Unvalidated change evidence reaches both termini, and is densest at the
   clipped one.** 46,245 candidate pixels (4.624 km²) lie inside the corridor;
   the closest is 0.12 km from the clipped south-western end, and the highest
   candidate density of any 5 km band is the band that the cut terminates
   (686 px/km² against 157–438 elsewhere) (§3.5). This is **not** a basis for
   choosing the extent (§3.5.3) but it does show the cut is not sitting in an
   area where the observation is empty.

### 1.3 Recommendation in one paragraph

Derive the corridor from **the main stem only** — a single topological path
through HydroRIVERS, no tributaries, no "everything within the buffer" rule —
running from **the Nepal–China border crossing of the Bhote Koshi** (≈ 28.28 N,
upstream terminus) downstream to **the last main-stem point the event-window
same-track pair can still observe after insetting for the buffer and a 250 m
margin** (downstream terminus; along the present terminal bearing the covered
limit is **84.84142 E, 27.70118 N**, 27.4 km downstream of the current clipped
end, §4). Buffer with **flat caps at 1,000 m** (§5) after an explicit
reprojection to **EPSG:32645**, where the measured scale-factor error at 1 km is
**0.092 m** (§5.2). Expected result: **~94–98 km of centreline, ~188–196 km²**
at a 1 km buffer (§4.5), i.e. **+40–48 %** area, all inside the existing pair's
footprint so no re-selection is forced. The two parameters that need a human
decision before any geometry is written are the **upstream terminus** (§8.1) and
the **tributary policy** (§8.3); the buffer (§8.4) needs sign-off of the
sensitivity set.

* The raster footprint is *circular evidence*: it was derived from

* It is nevertheless a **useful negative control**. Any new corridor must be
  checked against the *scene* footprint, never against this crop.
* The crop ratio also quantifies the cost of the envelope-based working grid:
  only 8.09 % of the analysed rectangle is study area. That is an acquisition
  and compute cost, not a scientific error, and it scales with the AOI's
  envelope rather than its area (§7.2).

### 3.3 How much room there actually is to extend

Marching outward from each terminus of the *existing* corridor, along the
corridor's terminal axis, until the point leaves the common footprint:

| Direction | Axis bearing | Room before leaving the pair's footprint | Exit point |
|---|---|---|---|
| Upstream (NE terminus) | 32.5° | **155.7 km** | — |
| Downstream (SW terminus) | 235.4° | **27.4 km** | **84.84142 E, 27.70118 N** |

The downstream room is strongly direction-dependent, because the pair's swath
is a diagonal band whose lower edge rises eastwards:

| Bearing from the SW terminus | Room | Exit point |
|---|---|---|
| 180° (due south) | 11.85 km | 85.06939 E, 27.73809 N |
| 200° | 13.4 km | 85.02303 E, 27.73074 N |
| 220° | 17.9 km | 84.95271 E, 27.71926 N |
| **235°** (corridor's present terminal axis) | **27.1 km** | 84.84536 E, 27.70171 N |
| 250° | 63.3 km | 84.46840 E, 27.63998 N |
| 270° (due west) | 91.6 km | 84.13860 E, 27.82887 N |
| 290° | 102.0 km | 84.08703 E, 28.14233 N |

The search marches in 50 m steps, so each figure is accurate to ± 0.1 km. The
westward direction is bounded by the footprint's western corner rather than by
its lower edge, which is why the room does not keep growing westwards: it peaks
in the 270–290° sector and is shortest towards the south. **The river's real
course, not this table, decides the downstream limit** — which is why §6 makes
the downstream terminus a rule evaluated against actual reach geometry rather
than a number fixed today.

### 3.4 What the corridor's own geometry says about the existing reach

| Property | Measured |
|---|---|
| Area (EPSG:32645) | 132.9284 km² |
| Rings | 1 exterior, **0 holes**; valid; 1,515 coordinates (1,514 segments) |
| Connected | yes — all 53,170 cells of a 50 m rasterisation reachable from the SW end |
| Centreline proxy (area / 2 km) | 66.46 km; half-perimeter 66.758 km (0.44 % apart) |
| Terminus-to-terminus geodesic distance | 57.85 km → sinuosity proxy **1.149** |
| Longest segment | **2,803.48 m**, index 1513 = the ring's closing segment, bearing **341.71°** |
| Chord endpoints (EPSG:4326) | 85.05814, 27.86890 → 85.06750, 27.84501 |
| Longest segment within 3 km of the NE terminus | **200.02 m** |
| Longest segment within 3 km of the SW terminus | 2,803.48 m |

Interpretation, stated as inference and not as record:

* A round buffer cap produces dense short chords (median segment 78.27 m) and no
  long straight run. The **NE terminus is consistent with a natural buffer
  terminus**: nothing straight there.
* A flat (butt) cap on a 1 km buffer would be exactly **2,000 m** across. The
  SW terminus carries a **2,803.48 m** straight chord, 1.40× that, whose bearing
  is neither a meridian nor a parallel nor perpendicular to the corridor axis.
  The corridor was therefore **buffer-then-cut at its downstream end**, by a
  boundary that no artifact in this repository names (M6.7 §4.4 reached the
  same conclusion from the same measurement; this report adds the bearing and
  the upstream negative control).
* The polygon's westernmost vertex is one end of that chord; its southernmost
  vertex (85.074752, 27.843973) lies 0.025° east of it. So the removed area is a
  wedge at the south-west corner, not a clean cross-section.
* This is exactly the kind of feature that must **not** be inherited: an
  unexplained cut defines the downstream end of every exposure and connectivity
  number computed inside it.

One further cross-check, from M6.7 §4.3 and independently confirmed by this
report's connectivity result: the polygon erodes to 14 residual lobes at 1,000 m
with a maximum inscribed radius of 1,200.69 m. That is the expected signature of
a 1 km buffer of a **branching** network meeting at an interior angle ≈ 112°
(`1000 / sin(θ/2)`), not of a single line. The unverified corridor was built from
**two branches** — as its own `rivers` property claims ("Bhote Koshi River",
"Trishuli River") — and any replacement must state its branch policy explicitly
(§6.4).

#### 3.5.1 What this does and does not mean

It is **unvalidated change evidence**: M6.3's own provenance says the candidates
are "rule-based Sentinel-1 RTC γ⁰ change evidence, not confirmed flood or
damage", the rule is a fixed −3 dB dual-polarisation darkening test, and the
same mask contains 22.96 km² of candidates *outside* the corridor (slope,
shadow, land-cover and village change are not separable by this rule).

#### 3.5.2 The one thing it does establish

Change evidence **reaches both ends of the corridor**, and its density is
*highest* in the band terminated by the unexplained cut. The cut therefore does
not fall where the signal ends. That is a reason to treat the existing
downstream termination as suspect — nothing stronger.

#### 3.5.3 Why it must not select the extent

Choosing a study extent by where the signal was observed, then measuring the
signal inside it, is selection on the outcome: it inflates every downstream
exposure and connectivity count and destroys the ability to report a negative
result. The candidates are **diagnostic only**. They may be used, as here, to
characterise truncation risk; they may not define the AOI, set its buffer, or
tune its endpoints.

## 4. Recommended extent, with the evidence for each element

### 4.1 Upstream terminus — **rule specified, choice requires ratification**

**Recommended rule.** Terminate the corridor at the **main-stem crossing of the
Nepal–China boundary** — i.e. the upstream-most main-stem reach inside Nepal.
Evaluate at construction time from the pre-event OSM boundary (a permitted
source, `AGENTS.md` §3) intersected with the selected HydroRIVERS main stem;
record the reached id and the crossing coordinate produced by that operation.

Justification:

* The case study is a **Nepal** disaster. The corridor's purpose is
  infrastructure exposure and connectivity for communities that can be cut off;
  upstream of the border there are no Nepali settlements in the study scopes
  named anywhere in the repository.
* The event description ("avalanche of ice and rock followed by flood water, mud
  and debris") is consistent with a transboundary source region, so the border
  is the **natural hydrological entry point of the event into the study area**
  rather than an arbitrary length cap.
* It is **reproducible and traceable** in a way a length cap is not: the
  boundary is a documented feature, and the resulting coordinate is a derived
  value with a stated derivation.

**What is *not* established:** the border's coordinate on this river. No local
dataset contains it. The existing corridor's NE terminus sits at
`85.384313, 28.284868` and is *consistent* with a border-crossing terminus, but
it is a fact about an unverified file, not evidence of intent — it must not be
reused as the answer. Recommended: adopt the rule, let the construction stage
compute the coordinate, and record it.

**Alternative that must be rejected or explicitly chosen (§8.1):** extend into
the Tibetan headwater to include the avalanche source reach. It would add
tens of km of corridor and change the study from "Nepal's flood" to "the event's
whole source-to-damage path". Defensible, but a scope decision, not a
measurement.

### 4.2 Downstream terminus — **rule specified, mechanism evaluated at construction**

**Recommended rule (coverage-bounded, deterministic):** walk the main stem
downstream and stop at the **last reach whose 1 km-buffered, flat-capped
geometry lies entirely inside the common footprint of the selected same-track
pair, inset by an additional 250 m margin**. Record the terminus coordinate, the
reached id, the pair used, and the fraction of the resulting AOI covered by each
scene.

Justification and its cost, stated plainly:

* A flood extent read from a scene is bounded by the image edge, not by the
  flood (`docs/m6-acquisition-validation.md` §2, §6). Ending the corridor before
  the image edge is the only way the study area's downstream end is a
  *scientific* end rather than an *instrument* end.
* It is deterministic given the pair and the constructed line, so two
  independent runs agree bit-for-bit.
* **Cost:** the AOI becomes coupled to a specific scene pair. If the pair is
  ever re-selected (a lower-cloud post-event scene, a longer window, a different
  orbit), the swath changes slightly and the terminus rule must be re-evaluated.
  Record the coupling explicitly in provenance and re-check coverage whenever
  the pair changes. An alternative that avoids the coupling — a fixed maximum
  main-stem length — replaces a data-availability boundary with an arbitrary
  one, which is worse; the third option is to stop deliberately short at a
  hydrological feature (§4.4, Option C).

**Supporting measurements:** along the present terminal axis the covered limit is
`84.84142, 27.70118`, 27.4 km downstream of the current clipped end. Measured
room from that end ranges from 11.85 km (due south) to ≈ 102 km (290°, where the
footprint's western corner bounds it), so the rule's outcome depends on which way
the river runs — which is precisely why it is stated as a rule and not as a
coordinate (§3.3).

### 4.3 Branch and tributary policy — **requires a human decision**

Recommended: **main stem only.** Include exactly the reaches on a single
topological path from the upstream terminus to the downstream terminus. No
tributaries; no automatic inclusion of reaches that happen to fall inside the
buffer.

Why this is the conservative default:

* It produces a **single simple path**, which buffers to **one** polygon — and
  the AOI loader accepts only one (§6.6). Branching selections can dissolve into
  a `MultiPolygon`, which the loader refuses by design.
* It is the **smallest** area that satisfies the requirement, so exposure counts
  are not inflated by tributary valley floors that the event did not traverse.
* It makes the corridor's identity auditable: a list of reach ids forming a
  path, plus two termini.

Branches that this excludes and that a reviewer will ask about: the second
headwater branch above the first junction (the existing polygon's geometry
implies at least one interior junction, §3.4), and any large tributary joining
inside the reach. Including them adds length at a rate of **2.0 km² of AOI per
km of centreline** at a 1 km buffer (measured, §4.5).

### 4.4 Plausible extent choices compared

| | **A — Coverage-bounded main stem** (recommended) | **B — Hydrological completeness** | **C — Deliberate truncation for comparability** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Upstream terminus | Nepal–China border crossing | Border, or headwater source reach | Border |
| Downstream terminus | Last reach inside the pair's common footprint (§4.2) | A fixed downstream hydrological junction below the swath (e.g. the first confluence with a higher-order river, or the mountain-front break) | ≈ 62–66 km along-channel, chosen explicitly for comparability |
| Centreline length | **~94–98 km** (§4.5) | **> 105 km**; downstream reach outside orbit-85 coverage | ~66 km |
| AOI area @ 1 km buffer | ~188–196 km² | > 210 km² | ~132 km² |
| Same-track pair | Unchanged (85A, 08-16 / 08-28) | **Forced change**: needs an orbit-19 post-event scene (outside +3 days) or a cross-track comparison | Unchanged |
| Coverage gate (≥ 0.9) | Passes by construction | Cannot pass for the downstream part without new scenes | Passes |
| Main scientific gain | Removes an unexplained truncation; keeps the observation complete to the instrument limit | Complete hydrological context to the plains | Direct comparability with M6.1–M6.3 results already computed |
| Main scientific cost | Its downstream end is an instrument boundary and must be disclosed as one | Breaks the same-track rule, or adds ≥ 8 days of post-event mismatch; crosses into a different flood regime (bank overflow / sedimentation in the lower valley) | Retains a truncation with a *stated* reason but no hydrological one; the event's downstream reach may be cut mid-signal |
| Verdict | **Recommend** | Reject unless a new post-event descending scene is acquired, with the temporal cost recorded | Acceptable fallback if the project prioritises comparability; the truncation must be documented per §4.4.1 |

#### 4.4.1 If Option C is chosen

It is only defensible if the truncation is (a) stated in the AOI provenance and
the dashboard, (b) *not* the existing chord — the terminus must be a
cross-section perpendicular to the local flow direction, and (c) accompanied by
a sensitivity check showing how much of the M6.3 candidate evidence lies within
the last 5 km and would be excluded. On the current evidence that last check is
material: the 0–5 km band at the clipped end carries the **highest** candidate
density of any band (686 px/km², 16.2 % of all in-corridor candidates, §3.5).

## 5. Buffer distance and CRS

### 5.1 Buffer distance — **1,000 m recommended, re-derived, with a sensitivity set**

The existing file's `buffer_meters: 1000` is a declaration with no justification
(M6.7 Q4) and is **not** inherited here. Re-derived, the value rests on three
independent arguments and one floor:

| Argument | Basis |
|---|---|
| **Floor — source positional uncertainty.** HydroRIVERS is "extracted from the gridded HydroSHEDS core layers at 15 arc-second resolution" (official product page). 15  arc-seconds is **460.7 m** in latitude and **409.5 m** in longitude at 28.0° N. A buffer narrower than one source cell can exclude the very channel the corridor is supposed to contain. | measured cell size; product documentation |
| **1,000 m ≈ 2.2 ×** that cell size in latitude, so the AOI contains the channel belt and the immediate floodplain under any plausible positional offset of the HydroRIVERS line. | arithmetic on the above |
| **Semantics.** The corridor must contain the infrastructure whose *exposure* is being examined — roads that run along the valley floor, bridges, and the buildings nearest the river. A buffer that stops at the channel edge would systematically miss them. | project requirement, `README.md` §4 |
| **Counter-argument, stated.** Every extra 1 km of width adds roughly 2 km² of AOI per km of centreline and *inflates* every exposure count with features that are merely near the river. That is why a **sensitivity set is mandatory**, not optional. | measured scaling, §4.5 |

**Mandatory sensitivity set:** `{500, 1000, 2000}` m. Report for each: AOI area,
measured coverage of both pair scenes, the count of intersected roads / bridges
/ buildings (M6.4-style), and the unreachable-settlement count (M6.5-style).
This mirrors the project's existing sensitivity practice for the road-closure
threshold (`configs/candidate_disruption.yaml`, `sensitivity_closure_m`) and the
requirement in `AGENTS.md` §9 that disruption thresholds be tested; the AOI
buffer is currently covered by *no* such requirement, which this section adds.
The 500 m case is deliberately included at the source-cell floor so the floor's
consequence is visible rather than assumed.

**What the buffer is not.** It is not a flood extent, not a hydraulic margin,
not an inundation probability, and not derived from the event's observed flood
footprint. It is an **analysis extent**. Any dashboard or report layer must
label it as such (`AGENTS.md` §16: do not let the visual product imply a
physical claim the data does not support).

**Cap style: flat (butt), not round.** Round caps add a semicircular bulge of
≈ 1.57 km² at each terminus and push the AOI one buffer-radius *beyond* the
chosen endpoint — which, at a downstream terminus deliberately inset for
coverage, would defeat the inset. Flat caps make the corridor end exactly at the
declared cross-section and keep area linear in length.

### 5.2 CRS — **EPSG:32645 for construction**, with a measured justification

| Requirement | Measurement |
|---|---|
| Buffer distance must mean metres on the ground | EPSG:32645 meridional scale factor over the corridor's envelope: **0.99990848 – 1.00005136**. A 1,000 m buffer operation therefore yields **999.91 – 1,000.05 m** of ground width. Worst-case distortion at 1 km: **0.092 m**. |
| One projection must span the whole corridor | Corridor longitudes 85.058–85.388 E lie inside UTM zone 45N (84–90 E), 1.6–1.9° west of the 87° E central meridian. |
| Must agree with the analysis grid | The project's preprocessing grid is already EPSG:32645 at 10 m, and the raster crop's bounds reproduce the corridor's reprojected envelope to under 10 m (§3.1). |
| Must not be degrees | Buffering in EPSG:4326 would be anisotropic by **1.125×** (one km = 0.00904° latitude vs 0.01017° longitude), i.e. a visibly oval corridor — precisely the error `AGENTS.md` §7 forbids. |

A corridor-specific transverse Mercator (central meridian ≈ 85.2° E) would reduce
the residual below 1 cm/km, at the cost of a non-standard CRS that no other stage
uses. At 1 km the difference is 9 cm: **not worth the interoperability cost.**
Record the pyproj/PROJ version and the exact transformation used; EPSG:4326 →
EPSG:32645 is a projection change with no datum shift, which must still be stated
rather than assumed.

**Storage format follows the contract, not the construction CRS:** the AOI file
is delivered in **EPSG:4326**, because `AreaOfInterest` accepts only that
(`SUPPORTED_AOI_CRS`, `src/floodmap/acquisition/aoi.py`) and the provider spatial
filter is defined in it (`docs/data-contract.md` §0.1). Construction happens in
EPSG:32645; the export is projected back, with the round-trip recorded.

### 6.2 Selection and traversal

```text
Step 1  SEED
        seed = the reach nearest the project-declared reference point, within a
        stated tolerance (recommend ≤ 1,000 m). If no reach is within tolerance,
        STOP and report — do not silently take the nearest.
        Record: reference point, its provenance, seed HYRIV_ID, offset distance.

Step 2  UPSTREAM TRAVERSAL (to the declared upstream terminus)
        From the seed, move to the upstream neighbour(s) repeatedly, using the
        delivered schema's upstream/downstream linkage (a reach r is upstream of
        the current reach when r's downstream pointer equals the current id).
        Choice rule where a branch occurs: the neighbour with the larger
        upstream-contributing area (fall back to the higher stream order, then
        to the lower HYRIV_ID) — the rule must be fixed BEFORE the run and
        recorded with its tie-breaks.
        Stop at the first of:
          (a) the declared upstream terminus condition (Nepal–China boundary
              crossing), or
          (b) a reach with no upstream neighbour (a source), or
          (c) a traversal depth limit, used as a runaway guard only.
        Record every id, its attributes, and the stop reason.

Step 3  DOWNSTREAM TRAVERSAL (to the coverage-bounded terminus)
        From the seed, follow the downstream pointer repeatedly. After each
        step, buffer the accumulated path and test containment in the common
        footprint of the selected same-track pair, inset by 250 m, per §4.2.
        Stop at the last step that PASSES. Record the id, the terminus
        coordinate, and the coverage fraction of each scene in the pair.

Step 4  INTEGRITY CHECKS (fail, never repair)
        - the union of upstream + seed + downstream steps contains no repeated
          reach (no cycle);
        - exactly one reach has no selected upstream neighbour and exactly one
          has no selected downstream neighbour (i.e. the selection is ONE path);
        - the path is geometrically connected under the stated tolerance;
        - every step is monotone in the downstream-distance attribute.

Step 5  GEOMETRY PREPARATION
        Reproject the selected reaches EPSG:4326 → EPSG:32645 with the recorded
        pyproj/PROJ version and explicit always_xy conventions.
        Do not simplify. Do not call make_valid: an invalid delivered geometry
        is a finding to report, not something to guess a repair for.

Step 6  BUFFER
        union( reach.buffer(distance, cap_style='flat', join_style='round') )
        Record: distance, cap style, join style, quad_segs, shapely version
        (vertex count depends on quad_segs, so the value is part of the result).

Step 7  TERMINAL CROSS-SECTIONS
        Intersect the buffered union with a half-plane through each terminus,
        perpendicular to the local reach direction (± 90° from the terminal
        segment bearing). Record both cross-section bearings and end coordinates.
        FORBIDDEN clips: the old AOI or its bbox, the raster envelope, any
        administrative boundary not named in the decision record, any scene edge.

Step 8  VALIDATION
        is_valid, is_simple, exactly one Polygon (not MultiPolygon), closed ring,
        no self-intersection, area and perimeter recorded, holes listed and
        explained (a buffer of a branching path can legitimately enclose an
        island between two close meanders — record it rather than dissolving it
        away).

Step 9  EXPORT
        One RFC 7946 file in EPSG:4326 (lon/lat, 6–7 decimal places, stated
        rounding), as a bare Polygon, a Feature, or a single-feature
        FeatureCollection — the three containers the loader accepts.
        Write the derivation into the feature's `properties` (§6.3).
        Compute and record sha256 over the exported bytes.
```

### 6.3 Properties the exported AOI must carry

```text
aoi_id                 new, stable, e.g. trishuli-hydrorivers-mainstem-<hash8>
name                   must state: derived analytical corridor, NOT the
                       organizer-selected AOI (M6.8)
derivation_source      HydroRIVERS, version, source URL, retrieval date, sha256
licence / attribution  HydroSHEDS licence text + Lehner & Grill (2013)
algorithm_version      identifier of the construction stage
code_version           commit SHA at construction time
reach_ids              ordered list of HYRIV_ID values (or the delivered
                       identifier field), with attributes used for traversal
endpoints              upstream + downstream terminus coordinate, the
                       cross-section bearing, the reached stop-reason
buffer                 metres, cap style, join style, quad_segs
crs_operations         EPSG:32645 for construction, EPSG:4326 for delivery,
                       pyproj/PROJ versions
coverage               measured fraction for every scene of the selected pair
limitations            at minimum: instrument-bounded downstream terminus (if
                       Option A), unvalidated buffer distance, transboundary
                       upstream truncation, and that this is not the official AOI
```

Provenance must be attached with `floodmap.utils.provenance` so the mandatory
challenge attributions travel with the artifact (`AGENTS.md` §18).

### 6.4 Decisions the algorithm encodes, stated openly

| Decision | Encoded rule | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Branch policy | main stem only | determines whether the result is one Polygon and how much area is added |
| Branch choice at a fork | larger upstream area → higher order → lower id | without a fixed rule the traversal is not reproducible |
| Tributary inclusion | none | an "all reaches within the buffer" rule would make the AOI depend on the buffer, and would add valley floors the event did not traverse |
| Tributaries *near* the main stem | kept out unless a human names them | a fixed, minimal, auditable rule beats a distance heuristic |
| Confluences | handled implicitly by the union of buffered reaches; the union is locally wider there (the existing polygon's 1,200.69 m inscribed radius at θ ≈ 112° is this effect, §3.4) | no special geometry is needed, but the effect must not be mistaken for a buffer error |
| Clipping | only the two terminal cross-sections | an unexplained clip is the defect this stage exists to remove |
| End caps | flat | keeps the corridor inside the declared endpoints and keeps area linear in length |
| Validity | reject, never repair | `docs/m6-acquisition-validation.md` §6 |
| Simplification | none | vertex count changes geometry; the AOI is hash-pinned |

Two consequences worth stating before anyone runs it:

* **The crop is envelope-driven.** Extending the corridor 31 km downstream grows
  the *analysed rectangle* by ~52 % while growing the study area by ~45 %. There
  is no way around this with the current grid convention, but it should be a
  deliberate, recorded cost rather than a surprise.
* **Coverage must be re-measured, never inherited.** The M6.2 measurement is a
  measurement of the *old* polygon. The new AOI carries a new id and hash, and
  the 0.9 gate must be re-evaluated against it (`docs/m6-acquisition-validation.md`
  §4: the envelope may not be substituted for the polygon).

### 7.3 Downstream analysis

* **Exposure (M6.4-style):** counts scale roughly with corridor length × buffer
  width. Option A at 1 km adds ~45 % of corridor to the intersection search; the
  `{500, 1000, 2000}` m sensitivity set changes it by roughly ∓50 % / ±100 %.
  Because M6.4 is **blocked on permitted pre-event OSM** anyway, no existing
  result is invalidated by the change — but no existing exposure count may be
  re-labelled as belonging to the new AOI.
* **Connectivity (M6.5-style):** more corridor ⇒ more bridges and more
  road segments that can be marked disrupted ⇒ more settlements whose
  connectivity is evaluated. Also blocked on OSM. The semantic rules do not
  change: a candidate mask intersecting a road is **exposure**, not destruction
  (`AGENTS.md` §8).
* **A physical caveat that must travel with the lower reach:** below the
  mountain front the flood regime changes (bank overflow and sedimentation
  rather than a confined surge). Any reach added downstream by Option A must be
  interpreted with that in mind; the report must not present one
  flood-process story along the whole corridor.
* **Historic artifacts:** M6.2 (coverage, validation), M6.3 (change candidates),
  M6.4/M6.5 (blockers) and the dashboard bundle all reference
  `trishuli-corridor-UNVERIFIED-9035eeee`. They remain valid **as measurements
  of the old geometry** and must be regenerated or explicitly marked stale for
  the new AOI. Editing their recorded AOI id to match the replacement would
  manufacture agreement between two different study areas.

## 8. Unresolved questions and required human decisions

Every item below is a decision, not a missing computation. Items 8.1, 8.3 and
8.4 block geometry construction; the rest block promotion or acquisition.

### 8.1 Upstream terminus (blocking)

Is the corridor's upstream end the **Nepal–China border crossing** (recommended,
§4.1), the **headwater source reach** including the Tibetan source region, or a
shorter inland start? Record the choice, the reasoning, and the authority.

### 8.2 Downstream terminus (blocking, mechanism already fixed)

Ratify the **coverage-bounded rule** (§4.2) rather than a fixed coordinate, and
accept its cost: the AOI becomes coupled to the specific same-track pair, which
must then be re-checked if the pair ever changes. Alternatives: a fixed
hydrological junction below the instrument limit (Option B — requires new
scenes and a longer temporal window), or a deliberate truncation (Option C).

### 8.3 Branch and tributary policy (blocking)

Confirm **main stem only** (§4.3), or name the additional branches/reaches to
include. This is the single largest determinant of the new area after the
termini themselves.

### 8.4 Buffer distance and sensitivity (blocking for the final artifact)

Ratify **1,000 m** with the `{500, 1000, 2000}` m sensitivity set (§5.1), or
substitute another value with its own justification. The value is result
affecting; it needs an owner, in writing.

### 8.5 Provenance status for a derived corridor

`AreaOfInterest.authorises_download` returns `True` **only** for
`provenance_status: verified`. A derived corridor's provenance can be complete —
source product, version, hash, licence, algorithm, parameters, endpoints — even
though it is not "official". Decide whether that complete derivation may be
promoted to `verified` (with the disclosure text above made mandatory), or
whether a new status is required. **Do not** set `verified` while any of §8.1–8.4
is unanswered, and do not treat this report as the promotion record.

### 8.6 Third-party approval record for HydroRIVERS use

M6.8 recorded the HydroRIVERS route as **"conditional, human approval
required"**. This milestone's decision context states the project approved it,
but **no approval record exists in the repository**. Before the geometry stage
runs, record who approved the derivation, when, and on what terms — including
whether a HydroRIVERS-derived AOI is acceptable for a competition submission.

### 8.7 Operator identity

M6.7 §8 Decision 4 stands unresolved: no named individual or role holds the
"operator" authority for AOI decisions.

### 8.8 Acquisition authorisation for the HydroRIVERS download itself

Retrieving the ~91–103 MB Asia extract is a **new source acquisition**, distinct
from satellite acquisition and not covered by the existing gates. It needs its
own authorization, its destination is `data/raw/` (gitignored, `AGENTS.md` §15),
and its licence/attribution must be recorded. The existing gates —
`authorises_download: false`, `download.enabled: false`, exploratory exception
disabled, AOI status `unverified` — **must remain untouched** by the geometry
stage.
